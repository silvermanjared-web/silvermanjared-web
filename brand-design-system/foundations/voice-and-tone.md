# Voice and Tone

## Voice

Warm, clear, professional, and useful.

The brand should sound like a capable guide: calm, practical, direct, and human. It should avoid inflated language, empty enthusiasm, and generic corporate phrasing.

## Tone range

### Default

Clear, helpful, confident.

Example: `Find the right program for your student, then take the next step with confidence.`

### Supportive

Encouraging but grounded.

Example: `You do not have to figure it out alone. We will help you understand your options.`

### Action-oriented

Direct and low-friction.

Example: `Start your application. You can save your progress and come back later.`

### Reassuring

Calm, steady, and specific.

Example: `A team member will review your information and follow up with next steps.`

## Do

- Use plain language.
- Lead with the user benefit.
- Keep sentences tight.
- Make next steps clear.
- Use specific nouns and verbs.
- Write for real people, not internal stakeholders.

## Avoid

- Vague claims like `best-in-class`, `innovative`, or `transformative` without proof.
- Overly playful copy in serious decision moments.
- Dense paragraphs.
- Exclamation-heavy language.
- Jargon unless the audience expects it.

## Microcopy guidance

Buttons should use strong verbs and clear outcomes.

- Good: `Apply now`, `Schedule a tour`, `Request information`, `Compare programs`
- Avoid: `Submit`, `Click here`, `Learn more` when the destination is unclear
